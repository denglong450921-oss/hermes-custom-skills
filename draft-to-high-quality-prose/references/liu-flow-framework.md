# Liu Flow Framework

This reference adapts the eighth entry from Liu Junqiang's `Writing Is a Craft`, revised edition: the "flow" section, especially Chapter 23 "Words and Sentences: Microstructure" and Chapter 24 "Paragraphs and Sections: Mesostructure".

Use it to turn draft text into high-quality prose. The target is not prettiness. The target is a reader path that moves smoothly from idea to idea.

## Operating Principle

Assume the reader is busy, distractible, and reluctant to solve the author's structure problem. The writer must carry the organizing cost.

High-quality prose should make these visible:

1. clear purpose
2. concrete subjects
3. subject-verb-object trunk
4. sentence-to-sentence continuity
5. controlled sentence rhythm
6. one idea per paragraph
7. topic sentences and signposts
8. layout that helps the eye move

## Layer 1: Intention

Before rewriting, identify the draft's job:

- **Audience**: Who must understand this?
- **Purpose**: Inform, persuade, explain, move, summarize, sell, reflect, or argue?
- **Form**: Article, paper section, speech, memo, short post, script, report, email?
- **Tone**: Plain, sharp, warm, rigorous, literary, professional, public-facing?
- **Invariants**: Facts, terminology, citations, names, numbers, claims, author voice.

Ask internally: "What is this piece really trying to say?" Then rebuild around one dominant line. Remove or relocate anything that does not serve that line.

Use these checks:

- Can the whole piece be summarized in one sentence?
- Does every paragraph serve that sentence?
- Does every sentence serve its paragraph?
- Does the reader know why this sentence appears here?

## Layer 2: Word Choice

Use Liu's abstraction ladder: abstract words raise the level of thought, but too many abstractions create fog. Concrete words create pictures, but too many details can trap the prose.

Revision rules:

- Replace empty big words with concrete actors, objects, actions, variables, stakes, or scenes.
- Add a higher-level concept after a narrow example when the draft lacks meaning.
- Define, exemplify, or replace insider jargon.
- Ask "what kind?" when a noun is vague.
- Ask "how?" when a verb is vague.

Common vague nouns:

- 能力, 机制, 体系, 生态, 赋能, 闭环, 格局, 价值, 认知, 优化, 现代化

Common vague verbs:

- 推动, 促进, 提升, 实现, 打造, 加强, 构建, 赋能, 完善, 深化

Good revision often alternates concrete and abstract:

```text
concrete example -> abstract point -> concrete consequence
```

## Layer 3: Sentence Trunk

Every sentence needs a visible trunk: subject, verb, object or complement. Readers should not search for the predicate through modifiers.

Rules:

- Prefer concrete, short subjects.
- Prefer observable verbs.
- Put the subject and verb close together.
- Move long conditions, background, and qualifications after the main clause when possible.
- Avoid "本研究", "本文", "相关方面", "一定程度上" unless they carry real meaning.
- Avoid "是...的" when a direct verb works better.
- In Chinese drafts, restore missing subjects when ambiguity weakens accountability or logic.

Example:

```text
Before: 在多种复杂因素共同作用并持续演化的背景下，家庭教育中的沟通机制呈现出明显的失衡状态。
After: 家长和孩子越来越难好好说话。时间压力、成绩焦虑和手机使用一起改变了家庭沟通。
```

The rewrite names the actors, exposes the verb, and moves from observable trouble to mechanism.

## Layer 4: Sentence Flow

Fluent prose lets one sentence hand the reader to the next. Use sentence chaining.

Rules:

- Put old or familiar information first.
- Put new or difficult information later.
- Let the next sentence pick up the previous sentence's ending, keyword, object, or implication.
- Put concrete material before abstract interpretation.
- Use contrast only when it sharpens meaning.

Flow pattern:

```text
Sentence 1: Introduce object A.
Sentence 2: Use A to reveal problem B.
Sentence 3: Explain why B creates consequence C.
Sentence 4: Turn C into the paragraph's point.
```

If two adjacent sentences share no subject, keyword, causal link, contrast, or progression, build a bridge or reorder them.

## Layer 5: Rhythm

Long sentences carry relations, qualifications, and layered meaning. Short sentences create emphasis and rest. Use both.

Revision rules:

- Split long sentences when the reader must hold too many conditions in memory.
- Combine short sentences when the prose feels chopped into fragments.
- Use a short sentence after a long explanation to land the point.
- Avoid paragraphs where every sentence has the same length and shape.

## Layer 6: Active and Passive

Prefer active voice when the actor matters. Use passive or actorless construction when it serves the reader:

- the process matters more than the actor
- the receiver of the action is the focus
- passive construction improves sentence chaining
- the writer must keep subjective presence restrained

Do not apply "always active" as a rule. Apply reader focus as the rule.

## Layer 7: Paragraphs

A paragraph should do one main job. If it explains background, makes a claim, gives evidence, handles an objection, and opens a new topic, split it.

Paragraph rules:

- Put the bottom line near the top.
- Make the first sentence a topic sentence unless suspense or narrative form clearly requires otherwise.
- Keep subjects or keywords in a visible chain.
- Keep most paragraphs under 8-10 lines in normal display.
- Delete sentences that do not serve the paragraph's job.

Paragraph repair:

1. Write the paragraph's one-sentence purpose.
2. Move that sentence to the beginning or make it the topic sentence.
3. Group supporting sentences by role: explanation, evidence, example, consequence.
4. Reorder from known to new, concrete to abstract, simple to complex.
5. Split if a second purpose appears.

## Layer 8: Sections

Sections and paragraph groups usually relate in three ways:

- **Parallel**: several reasons support one claim.
- **Progressive**: each paragraph advances the argument one step.
- **Contrastive**: alternatives are compared or opposed.

Choose one relation and make it visible with headings, topic sentences, or signposts.

Use headings when they help readers:

- see the structure
- predict what comes next
- locate the part they care about
- skip without losing the map

Avoid decorative headings that sound clever but fail to summarize.

## Decision Table

| If the Draft Has... | Do This |
|---|---|
| Abstract nouns stacked together | Lower one level: name the actor, action, object, example, or measurable variable |
| Big claims with no texture | Add one concrete scene, number, mechanism, or contrast |
| Many examples but no point | Add a topic sentence that extracts the meaning |
| Long winding sentences | Expose the subject-verb trunk, then move conditions later |
| Choppy short sentences | Combine related sentences around cause, contrast, time, or object |
| Paragraphs that feel scattered | Give each paragraph one job and rebuild the subject chain |
| Paragraphs where the memorable point is buried | Extract the one valuable claim and make it the topic sentence, or tag it with a `💡 要点` callout after the paragraph |
| Paragraphs that start with details | Put the bottom line first |
| Abrupt transitions | Use sentence chaining: repeat, synonymize, point back, or carry a keyword forward |
| Bureaucratic style | Replace institution-speak with people, actions, stakes, and consequences |
| AI-flavored prose | Remove generic lists, inflated metaphors, symmetrical slogans, and empty intensifiers |
| Literary over-polish | Restore clarity before elegance |

## Anti-Patterns

- **词语膨胀**: using large abstract words to hide thin thinking
- **主语漂移**: changing actors every sentence until the reader loses the thread
- **动词虚化**: using actionless verbs such as "促进", "优化", "赋能", "推动"
- **句子堵塞**: separating subject and verb with long modifiers
- **段落臃肿**: making one paragraph do several jobs
- **主题句缺席**: forcing the reader to infer the point after all details
- **衔接断裂**: placing adjacent sentences together without relation
- **花式润色**: polishing surface style while structure remains broken
- **过度金句化**: making every sentence clever until the paragraph stops moving

## Output Formats

Default:

```markdown
## 改写稿
<rewritten prose>

## 关键改动
- <3-6 structural changes>

## 仍需确认
- <only if needed>
```

Diagnosis:

```markdown
## 诊断
- **主线**: ...
- **词语**: ...
- **句子**: ...
- **段落**: ...
- **篇章**: ...

## 建议改法
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...
```

Revision memo:

```markdown
## 改写稿
...

## 改写依据
| 层级 | 问题 | 改法 |
|---|---|---|
| 词语 | ... | ... |
| 句子 | ... | ... |
```

## Ready Prompt

```text
请按刘军强《写作是门手艺（修订版）》第八篇“流动”的方法改写下面的草稿，使它成为清晰、流畅、高质量的 prose。

目标：
1. 保留原意、事实、立场和必要术语，不编造信息。
2. 先找主线：判断这段文字到底要表达什么。
3. 在词语层面，降低空泛抽象词的密度；必要时用具体对象、动作、例子或机制替代。
4. 在句子层面，让主语具体且简短，主谓宾尽快衔接，动词尽量可观察、可复现。
5. 在衔接层面，让上一句自然引出下一句，旧信息在前，新信息在后。
6. 在节奏层面，长短句结合；拆开堵塞的长句，合并碎裂的短句。
7. 在段落层面，一段只说一个意思，第一句尽量是主题句，段内主语或关键词形成主题链。
8. 在篇章层面，用小标题、路标词或主题句串联，让读者不用猜结构。
9. 删除空话、套话、AI腔、过度金句化表达。
10. **标记段落要点**：改写后，为每段提取最有价值或最令人印象深刻的论点。优先让它成为主题句；如果要点埋在段落中，在段后用 `> 💡 **要点**: [一句话]` 标注。如果某段没有值得标记的要点，考虑删掉或合并该段。

输出：
## 改写稿
<给出改写后的正文>

## 关键改动
- <说明最重要的3-6项结构性改动>

## 仍需确认
- <只有在事实、读者、语气或缺失信息影响改写时列出>

草稿：
<粘贴草稿>
```
